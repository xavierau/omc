# Sales Playbook — Educating Customers on WhatsApp Marketing

Audience: OhMyClient sales team
Purpose: Set realistic expectations during the sales cycle so customers don't get their number flagged or dragged down our shared WhatsApp Business Account (WABA) — and so we don't lose accounts after the first quality incident.

---

## 1. What we're actually selling

We are **not** selling "send WhatsApp blasts to anyone you want".
We are selling a **compliant, opt-in customer engagement platform** that uses WhatsApp's official Business API.

The customer's business is sharing a regulated platform (Meta WhatsApp Cloud API) with us and with other tenants. That changes how the conversation should go.

Two sentences you can use verbatim with prospects:

> WhatsApp is not SMS. Meta actively monitors every message and rates the quality of each business sending. If a business sends content users don't want, Meta lowers their reach automatically — and in serious cases the number gets banned. Our job is to keep your number healthy so it stays profitable.

---

## 2. The three things every customer must understand

These are the talking points to drill in *before* signing. If they don't accept these, the deal is bad for both sides.

### 2.1 Opt-in is non-negotiable

- Every contact on their list must have **explicitly agreed** to receive WhatsApp messages from **their specific business**.
- "I have their phone number from POS / reservation system / loyalty card" is **not** opt-in.
- Required record per contact: source, date, what they consented to (marketing? utility? both?), and the business name they were shown.
- If they got the number for a different purpose (e.g. table reservation), they cannot use it for promotions without re-confirming consent.

### 2.2 Quality matters more than volume

- Meta scores every business sender Green / Yellow / Red every 6 hours.
- One blast that gets blocked or marked as spam can drop the rating instantly.
- A bad rating means: lower delivery, frozen tier upgrades, eventually a ban.
- **Personalised, useful, expected** content keeps the rating green. Generic "20% off" blasts to a cold list will turn it red.

### 2.3 Pacing — you cannot blast Day 1

- A new number starts with a daily limit (1,000 unique recipients per 24h after Business Verification).
- Even within that limit, **WhatsApp dampens new numbers** during their first 30 days — sending too much too fast triggers a quality drop.
- Realistic ramp: 50-100 messages on Day 1, building to thousands per day by Week 3-4 — not Day 1.

## 3. Discovery questions (ask before quoting)

Use these in the first call. The answers tell you whether this is a healthy deal or a future support nightmare.

| Question | Why we ask | Red flag answer |
|---|---|---|
| "How did you collect these phone numbers?" | Opt-in proof | "I bought a list" / "from a competitor" / "we just have all our customers' numbers" |
| "Did the customers know they'd get marketing on WhatsApp?" | Consent specificity | "No, but they like our food" |
| "When did they last interact with you?" | List freshness | "We have numbers from years ago" |
| "What kind of message do you want to send first?" | Content fit | "20% off everything this weekend" |
| "How often do you plan to send?" | Frequency expectation | "Every day" / "Multiple times a day" |
| "Do you currently use SMS or email marketing? What's your open rate?" | Engagement baseline | Low single digits = poor list health |
| "Have you used WhatsApp marketing before? What was the outcome?" | Past incidents | "Our last number got banned" → tell them why this will happen again unless they change behaviour |

## 4. When to disqualify

It is **cheaper to lose a deal than to lose our shared WABA's reputation**. Politely disqualify if:

- They refuse to collect re-confirmation opt-in for an old list.
- They want to send to scraped or purchased contacts.
- They want to send daily promotional blasts.
- They have already had a number banned and won't change behaviour.
- Their content concept is generic price-discount blasts with no personalisation.
- They expect us to "find a way around" Meta's rules.

Escalation path: any disqualify decision should be logged so we have a paper trail if they later complain.

## 5. Common myths to debunk

| Customer says | The truth, plain English |
|---|---|
| "It's my number, I can send what I want." | The number is on Meta's platform. Meta sets the rules. Break them and Meta turns the number off — we cannot override that. |
| "I have all my customers' phone numbers from POS, that's enough." | Having someone's number isn't permission to message them on WhatsApp. Permission must be explicit and recorded. |
| "We did this on SMS for years and it was fine." | WhatsApp is not SMS. Users actively block and report. Meta uses those signals against you in real-time. |
| "Just give me the highest tier so I can blast." | Tier is earned through quality, not bought. Blasting before the number is warmed up will *lower* your tier, not raise it. |
| "Other agencies promised they can do this." | They are either lying, breaking the rules, or using grey-market tools that get the number banned within weeks. We won't do that. |
| "What if I just buy a new number when one gets banned?" | Meta tracks the business behind the number. New numbers from the same business inherit the bad reputation. The ban follows you. |

## 6. Pricing conversation: utility messages cost less than marketing

Meta charges differently per category. This is a powerful sales angle:

- **Utility** (order updates, reservation confirmations, loyalty point balance) — cheaper, no per-user limit, doesn't hurt quality rating.
- **Marketing** (promotions, offers, new menu) — more expensive, capped per user per day, hurts quality if abused.
- **Service / Authentication** — different rate cards.

Frame it: "Most of your value comes from automated utility messages. We'll structure your campaigns so 70% of your sends are utility — that protects your reach and keeps your bill down."

This is also healthier for the WABA. Sales should sell the utility-first model.

## 7. Setting expectations: realistic timeline

Use this slide / one-pager when closing:

| Phase | Timeline | What happens | Volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| Setup | Week -2 to 0 | Number provisioned, opt-in flow built, contacts cleaned | 0 sends |
| Probe | Days 1-3 | First sends to most-engaged 50-100 customers, utility content | 50-100/day |
| Build | Days 4-7 | Expand to bigger segment, mix utility + light promo | 200-500/day |
| Scale | Weeks 2-3 | Personalised promos, segmented | 1k-5k/day |
| Full | Week 4+ | Full broadcasts within tier limit | up to tier limit |

**The customer must understand they will not blast everyone on Day 1.** If they don't accept this, they're not a fit.

---

## 8. Red flags during the sales process

If you see any of these mid-cycle, escalate to the platform team before contracts are signed:

- Customer keeps insisting on a "test blast to everyone".
- Customer wants to upload a CSV with no opt-in metadata.
- Customer mentions a previous BSP / number / agency that got banned and wants us to "do it differently this time".
- Customer wants to send identical content to thousands of users with no personalisation.
- Customer wants no opt-out language in messages.
- Customer is in a high-risk vertical (gambling, adult, crypto, get-rich-quick) — these are usually outright prohibited by Meta.

---

## 9. One-page summary to leave with the customer

> **Your WhatsApp number is a regulated communication channel.**
> 1. We can only message customers who explicitly opted in to your business — recorded with date, source, and consent type.
> 2. Quality is graded by Meta in real time. Bad content = lower delivery → ban. Good content = higher reach → growth.
> 3. New numbers must be warmed up over 3-4 weeks. Day 1 is for 50-100 messages, not thousands.
> 4. Utility messages (order updates, reservations) are cheaper and safer than promotions. Mix both.
> 5. Always offer an opt-out. Always send messages users actually want.
> 6. We monitor your quality 24/7 and will pause sends if your rating drops — to protect your number.
> Followed properly, this turns WhatsApp into your highest-ROI channel. Ignored, the number gets banned.

---

## 10. Internal handoff checklist (sales → CS)

Before the deal is closed, confirm in writing with the customer:

- [ ] Opt-in source documented for all contacts being imported.
- [ ] Customer agrees to the warmup ramp (no Day-1 blast).
- [ ] Customer agrees to utility-first content mix for first 30 days.
- [ ] Customer's first three campaigns are co-designed with our CS team.
- [ ] Customer signs the platform usage policy (covers opt-out, content rules, quality auto-pause).
- [ ] If contact list is large (>5,000) or vertical is high-risk, platform team has approved the deal.

Hand off to CS only after all six are checked.
